What DejaView does

Ordinary duplicate finders compare names, sizes and checksums, so a 1080p file and its 720p re-encode look completely unrelated to them. DejaView compares what the frames actually show, which lets it match the same footage across different resolutions, formats, trims and black padding — even when the filenames have nothing in common.

Requirements

  • macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later
  • Apple silicon or Intel
  • No account, no internet connection needed

Getting started

  1. Click Add a Folder and choose a folder to search. Subfolders are included automatically.
  2. Click Scan for Duplicates. Groups appear as they are found — you don't have to wait for the whole scan.
  3. Open a group to compare every copy at the same moment of the footage, with the detected time offset applied.
  4. Review what's selected, then remove it. The full guide is inside the app under Help › DejaView Guide.

Common questions

Is anything deleted permanently?

No. DejaView never erases a file. Removals go to the Trash, where macOS lets you put them back, or into a quarantine folder beside the original if you prefer that. Every removal is logged first, and one copy in each group is always kept and is never selected for you.

Why is the first scan slow?

Decoding video is thousands of times more expensive than reading a filename. The first pass has to look at the footage itself. Results are cached, so a later scan of the same library is nearly instant, and files that are byte-identical are matched without decoding anything at all.

Can I stop a long scan and come back to it?

Yes. Pause saves a resume point, and you can quit the app entirely. Reopen it and the overview offers the unfinished scan. A resume point is also written every thirty seconds while scanning and when you quit, so a crash or a restart costs at most about half a minute of work — never the whole run.

A 480p file was picked as the keeper. Why?

It shouldn't be, and DejaView actively guards against it: it measures real picture detail so an upscaled copy can't win just because it has more pixels. If you're seeing this, check Settings › Keeper — the rule order and the preferred-resolution target are both adjustable, and you may have a target set that prefers a smaller copy. If the settings look right, please write to us with the details.

Why can't DejaView see one of my folders?

The app is sandboxed, so it can only read folders you have explicitly chosen in the folder-picker. Use Add a Folder to grant access. If a folder lives on an external drive that wasn't connected at launch, plug it in and add it again.

It found matches I don't think are duplicates.

The matching thresholds are adjustable under Settings › Matching. Raising the required containment makes the app stricter. Nothing is removed without your say-so, so a false match costs you nothing but a glance.

Reporting a problem

The most useful thing you can send us is the app's own diagnostics file. In DejaView:

  1. Open Activity Log from the menu bar (or press ⌘L).
  2. Switch to the Technical log tab.
  3. Click Save Diagnostics… and choose where to save it.
  4. Attach that file to your email.

It contains your settings, the technical log, and the crash report if there is one — which is usually enough to identify the problem without a back-and-forth. It may include the paths of files that were scanned, so have a look at it first if that matters to you. It is never sent anywhere automatically.
